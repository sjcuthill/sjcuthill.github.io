Hi Again!

Here's a few things you'll probably find me doing when I'm not working behind a computer.

I grew up with an acre of woods in my backyard along with my cousins and brother who were convinced it was much bigger than it actually was. To us, it was a whole forest. We built forts, explored every corner, and spent entire afternoons outside. And these weren't exactly the kind of forts you could throw together in five minutes. We were cutting branches, building walls, making doors, and trying to make something that would actually stay standing till the next summer.

I think I've always been like that.

I like making things, figuring things out, and doing things well. I like having something to work toward, whether that's finishing a new trail, sinking my teeth into a new project, learning a new dance, finding my next favorite book, helping the people close to me, or planning my next adventure.

Being outside has always been one of my favorite ways to reset. I love hiking, camping, exploring national parks, and finding places I've never been before. I find peace in the grandeur of nature and in the fact there are things bigger than myself. There is also something about setting out on a 12 mile hike up a mountain with just a backpack and my own 2 legs that is freeing.

Fair warning: hike with me and I'll probably convince you to take the harder trail for the better view.

Music has always been a pretty constant part of my life. Some of my fondest memories are my dad quizing me on who sings each song that came up on his playlist and random facts about them. I always have music playing. In the car, while I'm getting ready, while I'm cooking, while I'm working, pretty much whenever there's an opportunity. And above all, I love planning and dragging my friends to live music and concerts whenever I can.

Line dancing is probably one of the more unexpected things I've ended up loving. What started as something fun quickly became something I genuinely look forward to every Wednesday. I love learning new dances, remembering the steps, and eventually getting to the point where I don't have to think about them anymore as I idly do them while talking to my roomates in my house.

I've been slowly building a collection of books for years. I love having actual books on my shelves, especially when they have bent edges and little signs that they've been read and loved. Most of the books on my shelves have been read at least twice, with twice as many waiting beside them for their turn. Of course, that doesn't stop me from buying another one before I've finished the ones I already have.

Reading is probably the simplest way I've found to stay curious. I like getting completely absorbed in someone else's world and coming away with a new perspective.

Also, I have absolutely no interest in becoming a Kindle person.

A lot of the things I love come back to the people I get to do them with.

My family, my cousins, my friends, the people I meet along the way are a huge part of what makes all of these things meaningful. I like adventures, but I like having someone to share them with even more.

Some of my favorite memories aren't necessarily about where I was or what I was doing, but who was there with me. The thing I value most in life is having people around me who make me laugh, challenge me, support me, and are always willing to make a memory out of whatever we happen to be doing.

I have always been someone who says yes to things and been a little bit of a trailblazer. Whether it's jumping out of a plane first while skydiving, being the test dummy on the sled jump my dad made when I was a child, cliff jumping from the highest ledge, or being the first to introduce myself to a stranger, I like to think embarrassment is an unexplored emotion. I like learning something I've never done before, going somewhere I've never been, and picking up hobbies that I probably didn't need but am very glad I tried. Some things stick, some don't, but I would much rather try something and decide it's not for me than wonder what it would have been like.

I don't think every moment needs to be productive or have a bigger purpose. That means saying yes to the road trip, winning the dare to see who can get the most high fives from strangers in five minutes, staying out a little later, going to the concert, starting the hike at 3 a.m. to make it to the top by sunrise, and making plans just because they sound fun.

I don't think I've changed all that much since those forts in the backyard.

I still like exploring. I still like making things. I still like learning something new just because it's fun. And I still like taking an idea and seeing if I can make it work.

The forts just got a little more complicated.
