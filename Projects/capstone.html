EcoLoop

Whitman School · Syracuse University

A random team of five, one novel product idea, and a 15-minute pitch in front of real-world business judges—plus live questions on validity, how it works, and the numbers.

01 · The brief

Five strangers, one venture

For EEE 457 at Syracuse’s Whitman School, we were thrown into a random team of five and asked to invent something new—then prove it could hold up as a business. Not a class project that only needed slides: a full business profile, a working story for the product, and a live defense.

Our team: Jack Foster, Daniel Arougheti, Nathan Daniels, Sophia Russo, and me. I was team captain and project manager—organizing the group, keeping everyone on task, and delegating work so the project actually moved—while also serving as COO for operations, analytics, and the web side of how we’d show the brand online.

02 · The product

EcoLoop Filtration Solutions

EcoLoop is a design-forward planter that folds air purification, dehumidification, automated plant irrigation, and a self-powering hydrogen conversion loop into one appliance for small, poorly ventilated apartments.

The dehumidifier pulls moisture from the room and stores it to water the plant. HEPA and activated carbon filters capture dust, pollen, mold, and other particles. A two-chamber membrane system—developed with academic guidance—converts captured particle waste into hydrogen using sunlight, so the device can power itself while the condensed water keeps the plant alive. One object instead of a stack of single-purpose machines eating floor space and airflow.

We backed that with a full plan: industry and market analysis, marketing, operations and sourcing, financials, risks, and a proposed raise—targeting urban renters 23–35 who want cleaner air without another bulky box in the corner.

03 · The pitch

Fifteen minutes, five judges, no softballs

We had 15 minutes to present the idea to five judges from real businesses—then take live questions on the spot. They pushed on whether the product was valid, how the tech and operations would actually work, and financial questions we had to answer without hiding behind the slide deck.

That meant the business plan couldn’t be theater. Market sizing, unit economics, break-even, competitive positioning against brands like Dyson and Honeywell, and the ask all had to survive people who evaluate ventures for a living.

Outcome 2nd place in our room

04 · What I owned

Captain, PM, and COO

On a five-person founding roster that didn’t choose each other, someone had to make the machine run. As team captain and project manager I organized the work, delegated sections of the plan and pitch, chased deadlines, and kept people aligned so we weren’t five parallel drafts colliding the night before judges.

My functional lane was still COO—operations and measurement: how the venture would run day to day, how we’d track what mattered, and how the brand showed up digitally—including the EcoLoop site and marketplace mock.
