02 / Full Stack · Team Collaboration

CTV Buyer

Web Developer Intern · March 2025–Present

My current role: shipping full-stack pieces of a connected-TV buying platform with two other engineers—where the work isn’t only the feature, it’s the PR, the CI, and the environments that keep a shared product safe to change.

01 · The role

Limited agency, real ownership

Unlike New Leaf, I don’t set the product roadmap here. I work inside an existing codebase and process with a small team—two other people—where tickets, reviews, and release discipline matter as much as the code I write.

I’ve still owned substantial dashboard surfaces end-to-end. The through-line of this case study is that collaboration: how a feature moves from branch to review to the right environment without breaking what everyone else is building.

02 · How we ship

Process is part of the craft

On a three-person team, “done” means the change survives someone else’s eyes and the pipeline—not just that it runs on my laptop.

Pull requests

Features land as reviewable PRs with clear scope. Bigger work gets broken into commits that are easy to reason about—UI, worker scoring, migrations—so reviewers can follow the story.

CI checks

Nothing merges on vibes. Automated checks catch breakage before it hits shared branches, which matters when multiple people touch the same dashboard and data paths.

Environments

Dev and production stay intentional. Schema work is preflighted against origin/dev; duplicate or already-landed migrations get cleaned up so environments don’t drift.

Shared ownership

I build full slices when I own a ticket, but I still design for the next person reading the PR—naming, migration hygiene, and not leaving half-applied schema on someone else’s day.

03 · Featured build

Per-station Health Check

In CTV advertising, stations (publishers / inventory sources) continuously send bid requests—signals that there’s an ad opportunity available. Winning an auction isn’t enough: the creative has to render, and the station needs healthy inventory so buyers aren’t bidding into a dead pipe. Ops needed a way to see, per station, when those signals drifted from normal—before a campaign quietly under-delivered.

Problem

Station health was hard to spot at a glance. Bid-request volume, render rate, and inventory could dip for a day or a week without a clear baseline or alert surface. Admins needed thresholds, day-by-day context, and controls to hide or streamline noisy stations—not another raw metrics dump.

Solution

I built the admin Health Check page: each station is scored against a rolling baseline for bid requests, render rate, and inventory. A day heatmap shows status over the range; charts compare live performance to the baseline; flagged stations call out why (e.g. low bid requests for N days). Thresholds drive inventory alerting. Manage-stations and hide controls, plus a Streamline toggle, keep the board usable when the station list is long.

Scoring runs after daily aggregation in a worker—not on every page load—so the UI reads precomputed health instead of recomputing history in the request path.

  • Bid requests (BR) Volume of ad opportunities from the station, scored from channel_device_metrics_daily. A sustained drop usually means less supply reaching the auction.
  • Render rate (RR) How often won ads actually play. Separates “we bought it” from “the viewer saw it”—critical for diagnosing player or delivery issues vs. auction issues.
  • Inventory Health of available opportunities over time, with threshold limits so multi-day inventory problems flag louder than a one-day blip.
  • Pod clear rate (PCR) Σfilled / Σrequested from Publica auction totals (channel_pod_clear_daily, UTC). Shows how completely ad pods clear—ingest, worker pod_clear_rate, chart, and API wired through daily aggregation and backfill.
